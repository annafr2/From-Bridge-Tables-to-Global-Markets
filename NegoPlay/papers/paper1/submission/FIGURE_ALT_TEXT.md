# Alternative Text (Alt Text) for All Figures

Prepared per the Springer accessibility requirement ("please provide
suitable alternative text or descriptions for accessibility purposes").
All five figures are the author's own work, generated by the scripts in
the public repository; no third-party material. All figures are rendered
at print size (lettering 7 to 10 pt at the LNCS text width, above the
Springer minimum of 6 pt) and saved at 300 dpi.

---

**Fig. 1 (radar_profiles.png)**
Radar chart with six behavioral feature axes: slam rate, partscore rate,
no-trump rate, penalty-double rate, opening rate, and preempt rate, each
min-max normalized to the range zero to one across all players. Five
overlaid polygons represent the four extreme profiles and the dashed
Generalist baseline. Each profile's polygon spikes on its own defining
axis: Slam Hunter on slam rate, Insurance Player on partscore rate,
Fighter on penalty-double rate, NT Specialist on no-trump rate, and stays
close to the baseline on the other axes. A legend below the chart names
the five profiles.

**Fig. 2 (real_skill_spectrum.png)**
Two stacked panels sharing one measure, average IMPs per board relative
to the two-table datum. Top panel: a horizontal spectrum in which the
random ZI-C-style baseline sits at the far left at about minus 7 IMP per
board, the real elite players sit at zero, and the double-dummy oracle
sits at the right at plus 1.1 IMP per board; the ordering random, then
human, then omniscient validates the metric. Bottom panel: a zoom on the
five profiles, drawn as horizontal bars from zero: Slam Hunter plus 0.17,
Fighter plus 0.08, Generalist plus 0.01, Insurance Player minus 0.01, NT
Specialist minus 0.01.

**Fig. 3 (negotiation_example.png)**
Line chart of one negotiation scenario, acquiring a SaaS startup. The
horizontal axis shows negotiation rounds one to four; the vertical axis
shows the buyer's offer in millions of dollars. Three dashed reference
lines mark the seller's opening ask at 13, the fair value at 9, and the
seller's floor at 8. Five colored lines trace the agents' offers:
aggressive profiles anchor near the floor at 8 and hold; cautious
profiles concede upward toward 11.

**Fig. 4 (style_alignment.png)**
Scatter plot with five labeled points, one per profile. The horizontal
axis is bridge-side aggression as a composite z-score; the vertical axis
is negotiation-side demand depth. Points rise from the lower left
(Insurance Player, Generalist) to the upper right (Fighter), showing a
positive rank correlation of plus 0.80.

**Fig. 5 (style_transfer_control.png)**
Two stacked scatter panels for the inverse-prompt control, with the same
axes as Fig. 4. Top panel: in the matched condition, bridge aggression
and negotiation demand depth correlate at plus 0.80. Bottom panel: when
each profile keeps its label but receives the opposite profile's
bridge-derived skills, the correlation flips to minus 0.90; the Fighter
becomes softer and the Insurance Player becomes harder.

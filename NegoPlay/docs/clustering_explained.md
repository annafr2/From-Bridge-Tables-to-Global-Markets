## Figure 1 — The 8 features that go into K-Means

![The 8 clustering features](images/clustering_features.png)

**What you see:** the 8 behaviours we measure for every player, sorted by how
much they *separate* players (coefficient of variation = spread ÷ mean). A tall

## Figure 2 — Why K-Means FAILS

![K-Means silhouette across k](images/clustering_kmeans_fails.png)

**What you see:** we ran K-Means asking for 2, 3, 4, 5, and 6 groups. The red
line is the **silhouette score** — how cleanly separated the groups are.

**The rule:**
- silhouette ≥ **0.5** = real, well-separated clusters (green zone)
- silhouette ~0.25 = weak structure
- silhouette ~0.1 = essentially **no clusters**

**The result:** every single k scores around **0.11–0.14** — nowhere near 0.5.
No matter how many groups we ask for, K-Means cannot find clean ones.

**Why?** Because elite bridge players form **one smooth cloud (a continuum)**,
not separate islands. K-Means is built to find islands — so when there are none,
it just draws arbitrary lines through one cloud, and the silhouette stays low.

> The chart's footnote: this run uses the 8 raw features (no PCA) and lands at
> ~0.13. The best of our 5 preprocessing configurations (with PCA) reached only
> **0.24** — still far below the 0.5 needed. K-Means fails either way.

## Figure 3 — Why Extreme-Percentile SUCCEEDS

![Extreme-percentile tails](images/clustering_extreme_succeeds.png)

**What you see:** four mini-charts, one per profile. The grey histogram is the
whole population on that profile's defining behaviour; the coloured bars are the
profile's members. The dashed line is the **top-10% cutoff**.

**The idea:** instead of forcing the cloud into fake groups (what K-Means tried),
we keep the cloud as-is and simply **name its extreme tails**:
- **Slam Hunter** = the players in the top tail of `slam_rate`
- **Insurance Player** = top tail of `partscore_rate`
- **Fighter** = top tail of `penalty_double_rate`
- **NT Specialist** = top tail of `nt_rate`

Each coloured group sits cleanly in the right-hand tail — exactly where it
should. Every member also passes a **binomial significance test** (p < 0.05), so
we know their extreme score is real, not luck from a small sample.

**Why it works where K-Means didn't:** K-Means asks *"which island is this
player on?"* — wrong question, because there are no islands. Extreme-percentile
asks *"is this player in the extreme tail of a behaviour?"* — the right question
for a continuum.

## One-paragraph summary (for the supervisor)

> Elite bridge players do not split into discrete clusters — K-Means, HDBSCAN,
> and GMM all score silhouette ≤ 0.24, far below the 0.5 needed, because the
> data is a smooth continuum. We therefore replaced clustering with
> **extreme-percentile profiling**: each profile is defined as the statistically
> significant top tail (top 10%, binomial p < 0.05) of one behaviour axis. This
> is the correct tool for tail-finding in a continuum, and it is what produced
> the four validated profiles.
